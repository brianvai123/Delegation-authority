7.1.3 Delegation and Accountability 3 periods

The relationship between delegation and accountability

  • Delegation transfers authority to a subordinate, but accountability remains with the delegator.
  • Managers can delegate tasks, but they cannot delegate final responsibility.
  • Effective delegation requires clear expectations, resources, and follow-up.

The processes of accountability in a business

  • Setting clear performance standards and objectives.
  • Regular reporting, monitoring, and performance reviews.
  • Feedback, corrective action, and consequences for non-accountability.
  • Documentation and transparency in decision-making.

The impact of delegation on a business

  • Benefits: increased motivation, skill development, efficiency, succession planning, better time management for managers.
  • Drawbacks: loss of control, inconsistent quality, poor decisions if staff lack training, potential conflict.
  • Balancing delegation with accountability is key to positive impact.

7.1.4 Control, Authority and Trust 3 periods

The relationship between span of control and levels of hierarchy

  • Wide span of control → fewer levels of hierarchy (flat structure).
  • Narrow span of control → more levels of hierarchy (tall structure).
  • Trade-offs: closer supervision vs. faster communication and lower costs.

The difference between authority and responsibility

  • Authority = the right to give orders and make decisions (legitimate power).
  • Responsibility = the obligation to perform assigned duties and answer for outcomes.
  • Authority can be delegated; responsibility remains with the person who accepted the task.

The conflicts between control and trust that might arise when delegating

  • Too much control undermines trust and demotivates employees.
  • Too much trust without control risks errors, fraud, or goal misalignment.
  • Balancing: clear boundaries, milestone check-ins, and supportive feedback.
  • Cultural and situational factors influence the right balance.
